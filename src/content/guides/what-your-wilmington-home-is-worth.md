A price is not an opinion, it is an argument built from other people's closings. Here is how the argument gets built in New Hanover County, and where the automated estimates break down.

## The four adjustments that actually move the number

**Location tier.** Wilmington prices step in blocks, not zip codes. Sunset Park east of Carolina Beach Road and Sunset Park west of it are different markets, and a comparable sale from the wrong side of a corridor drags your number in the wrong direction.

**Condition versus the comp.** If the closed sale down the street had a 2023 kitchen and yours is 1998, that difference is subtracted at roughly 60% of the renovation's cost — not 100%. Buyers pay for finished, but they do not pay full retail for someone else's choices.

**Elevation and insurance.** On anything east of the Intracoastal or in flood zone AE, the annual premium a buyer will be quoted is effectively a deduction from your price. A $4,000-a-year premium consumes about $55,000 of buying power at current rates.

**Days of supply at your price.** Ten competing listings under $500,000 in your submarket is a different pricing environment than two. This is the variable automated tools ignore entirely, and it is the one that changes month to month.

## Why the Zestimate misses on the coast

Automated valuation models need dense, recent, similar sales. Barrier-island and historic-district properties fail all three tests: few sales, wide condition variance, and features (deeded slips, elevation, HPC restrictions) that have no field in the data model.

## What to do with your number

Price at the top of the band your comparable sales support, not above it. Wilmington buyers watch inventory daily, and a price cut in week three signals weakness in a way that a correct price in week one never does. Our listings went under contract in a median of 12 days last year, and every one of them launched inside the supported band.
