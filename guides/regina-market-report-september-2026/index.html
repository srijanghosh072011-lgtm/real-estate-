Regina Market Report: September 2026

Inventory is up 11% year over year and the median days on market has stretched to 24. Here is what that actually changes for buyers and sellers this fall.

By Avery Lindholm, Broker / Owner · September 9, 2026

On this page

  • The headline numbers
  • What it means if you are buying
  • What it means if you are selling
  • The rate picture
  • Our call for the fall

The headline numbers

Regina closed August 2026 with 501 active residential listings, up from 451 a year earlier. Median sale price held at $342,000, essentially flat year over year, while median days on market moved from 18 to 24. Sales volume was down 4% against a strong August 2025.

What it means if you are buying

More inventory and slower absorption means your negotiating position is better than it has been in two years, but only in the segments where inventory actually grew. Detached homes between $300,000 and $450,000 are still moving in under two weeks with multiple offers. The softening is concentrated above $600,000 and in one-bedroom condos. If you are shopping in the mid band, plan for competition; if you are shopping above $600,000, take your time and write conditions.

What it means if you are selling

The pricing mistake this fall is anchoring to a spring 2026 comparable. A home that would have drawn three offers in April will sit at the same number in October. Price against sales from the last 60 days, not the last 6 months, and be honest about condition. Homes that show well and are priced inside the comparable band are still selling in under three weeks.

The rate picture

Five-year fixed offerings at the major lenders sat between 4.19% and 4.64% through August. That is roughly a hundred basis points below the 2024 peak and is the main reason the entry band has stayed competitive despite higher inventory. Nothing in the current forward curve suggests a sharp move before year end.

Our call for the fall

A normal market, which after four abnormal years reads as a slowdown. Expect flat pricing, longer marketing times, and a return of the financing condition as a standard term rather than a deal-breaker. That is a healthier market for everyone who is not trying to flip.
