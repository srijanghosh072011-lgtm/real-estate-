The First-Time Buyer's Guide to Regina

Down payment rules, Saskatchewan land transfer costs, the inspection line items that matter here, and a realistic 60-day timeline from pre-approval to keys.

By Avery Lindholm, Broker / Owner · August 18, 2026 · updated September 1, 2026

On this page

  • What you actually need for a down payment
  • Saskatchewan-specific costs
  • The inspection items that matter in Regina
  • A realistic timeline
  • Programs worth knowing about

What you actually need for a down payment

In Canada the minimum is 5% on the first $500,000 and 10% on the portion between $500,000 and $1,500,000. On a $350,000 Regina home that is $17,500. Anything under 20% requires CMHC, Sagen or Canada Guaranty default insurance, which is added to your mortgage rather than paid up front. Budget an additional 1.5% of purchase price for closing costs.

Saskatchewan-specific costs

Saskatchewan has no land transfer tax. What you pay instead is a title registration fee scaled to property value, typically $200 to $400 on a median Regina home, plus legal fees of roughly $1,200 to $1,800 and a title insurance premium. This is meaningfully cheaper than buying in Ontario or BC and it surprises people who move here.

The inspection items that matter in Regina

Three things: the foundation, the shingles, and the electrical panel. Regina sits on expansive clay that moves with moisture, so any inspection should include a careful look at basement walls, floor cracks and door frames that no longer close square. Hail is frequent enough that shingle age is a real budget line. And in pre-1960 housing, knob-and-tube or a 60-amp panel will affect your insurability before it affects your comfort.

A realistic timeline

Week 1: mortgage pre-approval and a written needs list. Weeks 2 to 5: showings, usually eight to fifteen properties. Week 5: offer with financing and inspection conditions, typically a 7 to 10 business day condition period. Weeks 6 to 7: inspection, lender appraisal, condition removal. Weeks 8 to 10: lawyer, title transfer, possession. Sixty days start to finish is normal and rushing any of it is where people get hurt.

Programs worth knowing about

The federal First-Time Home Buyer Incentive has wound down, but the Home Buyers' Plan still lets you withdraw up to $60,000 per person from an RRSP tax-free toward a first home, and the First Home Savings Account allows $8,000 per year to $40,000 lifetime with both a deduction going in and tax-free growth coming out. The FHSA is the single best instrument available to a Saskatchewan first-time buyer and most people under thirty have not opened one.
