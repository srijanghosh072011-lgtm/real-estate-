The First-Time Buyer's Guide to Regina

Down payment rules, what closing actually costs in Saskatchewan, the inspection items that matter here, and a realistic 60-day run from pre-approval to keys.

By Avery Lindholm, Broker / Owner · August 18, 2026 · updated September 1, 2026

On this page

  • What you actually need for a down payment
  • What's different about closing in Saskatchewan
  • The inspection items that matter in Regina
  • A realistic timeline
  • Programs worth knowing about

What you actually need for a down payment

The minimum is 5% on the first $500,000 and 10% on anything between $500,000 and $1,500,000. On a $350,000 Regina house that's $17,500. Under 20% you'll need CMHC, Sagen or Canada Guaranty default insurance, which gets added to your mortgage rather than paid up front. Then budget another 1.5% of the price for closing.

What's different about closing in Saskatchewan

No land transfer tax. You pay a title registration fee scaled to property value instead, usually $200 to $400 on a median Regina house, plus $1,200 to $1,800 in legal fees and a title insurance premium. It's meaningfully cheaper than closing in Ontario or BC, and it surprises people who move here.

The inspection items that matter in Regina

Three of them. Foundation, shingles, electrical panel. Regina sits on expansive clay that moves with moisture, so any inspection needs a careful look at basement walls, floor cracks, and doors that no longer close square. Hail comes through often enough that shingle age is a real budget line. And in anything pre-1960, knob-and-tube or a 60-amp panel will hit your insurability before it hits your comfort.

A realistic timeline

Week one, pre-approval and a written needs list. Weeks two to five, showings, usually eight to fifteen houses. Week five, an offer with financing and inspection conditions, typically a 7 to 10 business day window. Weeks six and seven, inspection, appraisal, condition removal. Weeks eight to ten, lawyer, title, possession. Sixty days start to finish is normal, and rushing any of it is where people get hurt.

Programs worth knowing about

The federal First-Time Home Buyer Incentive has wound down. The Home Buyers' Plan still lets you pull up to $60,000 each from an RRSP tax-free toward a first house. And the First Home Savings Account gives you $8,000 a year to a $40,000 lifetime cap, with a deduction going in and tax-free growth coming out. The FHSA is the single best tool available to a Saskatchewan first-time buyer and most people under thirty still haven't opened one.
