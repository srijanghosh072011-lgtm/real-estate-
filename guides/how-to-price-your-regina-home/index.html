How to Price a Regina Home So It Actually Sells

Overpricing costs more than time. Here is the comparable method we use, why the first fourteen days decide the outcome, and what a price reduction really signals.

By Avery Lindholm, Broker / Owner · July 22, 2026

On this page

  • The first fourteen days are the whole game
  • How comparables should be selected
  • What a price reduction signals
  • The one exception to pricing at market

The first fourteen days are the whole game

A new listing gets its largest audience in the first two weeks. Every buyer already working with an agent in your price band sees it in that window. If it is priced above the band, they see it and pass, and you do not get a second first impression. Everything after day fourteen is scraps: new entrants to the market, plus the people who already passed and are now waiting for you to reduce.

How comparables should be selected

Same neighbourhood, same property type, sold within the last 90 days, within roughly 15% on square footage, and adjusted for garage, basement development, lot size and condition. Three to five genuine comparables beat fifteen loose ones. Active listings are not comparables, they are competition; a neighbour asking $475,000 tells you nothing except who you are bidding against.

What a price reduction signals

The first reduction reads as a correction. The second reads as motivation. The third reads as a problem with the property. Each one invites a lower offer than the last. This is why starting at the correct number beats starting high with a plan to come down: you spend the same weeks and end at a lower price.

The one exception to pricing at market

Deliberately pricing at the bottom of the comparable band to manufacture competition works in Regina, but only when inventory in your segment is genuinely tight and your property shows well enough to hold attention. We used it on 215 Tuscany View in April and it produced three offers in nine days and a sale $11,100 over asking. It is a strategy, not a default, and it fails badly on a property with a visible defect.
