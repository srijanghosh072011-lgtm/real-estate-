About Regina homes with basement suites

What makes a suite legal here

It has to meet the National Building Code as Regina has adopted it, and the City has to have permitted it. In practice that means a separate entrance, minimum ceiling height, egress windows in every bedroom, fire separation between units, interconnected smoke alarms, and enough ventilation and parking. Ask for the permit and the final inspection paperwork. If the seller can't produce it, treat the suite as unpermitted and price it that way.

How lenders count the rent

Most will use somewhere between 50% and 100% of documented suite rent toward your qualifying income, and it varies a lot between institutions. A legal, permitted, currently tenanted suite with a lease gets the best treatment. An unpermitted one often gets nothing at all. If suite income is what makes your purchase work, get the specific lender's policy in writing before you write an offer, not after.

What a suite rents for in Regina

A legal one-bedroom in a newer south-end neighbourhood runs roughly $1,100 to $1,250 with utilities. Two-bedrooms, and anything near the university or in Cathedral, go higher. Against a median mortgage payment that covers a serious chunk of the carrying cost, which is why suited houses draw multiple offers and rarely need a reduction.

What you're signing up for

You become a landlord under The Residential Tenancies Act. Rules about entry notice, deposits, rent increases, eviction. And someone else living in your building. Insurance changes too: a standard homeowner policy may not cover a rental suite, and not disclosing it can void a claim. Budget for a higher premium and actually read the policy.

Questions

Questions people ask

Are basement suites legal in Regina?

Yes, secondary suites are allowed where they meet building code and the City has approved them. Legality comes down to separate entrance, egress windows, fire separation, alarms, ceiling height, ventilation and parking. And to the permit actually existing.

How much does a basement suite rent for in Regina?

Roughly $1,100 to $1,250 a month with utilities for a legal one-bedroom in a newer neighbourhood. Two-bedroom and university-area suites go higher.

Will the bank count my basement suite rent?

Typically 50% to 100% of documented rent from a legal permitted suite, depending on the lender. Unpermitted suites often get ignored entirely. Get your lender's policy in writing before making an offer.

Does a suite affect my home insurance?

Yes. A rented secondary suite is a material change your insurer needs to know about. A standard homeowner policy may not cover it, and not disclosing can void a claim. Expect a higher premium and get the coverage confirmed in writing.
