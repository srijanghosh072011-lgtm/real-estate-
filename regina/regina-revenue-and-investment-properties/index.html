About Regina revenue & investment properties

The numbers we run before you write an offer

Purchase price and realistic rent for the unit type and neighbourhood, not the optimistic number in the listing remarks. Then property tax from the SAMA assessment, insurance quoted for a rental rather than an owner-occupied policy, condo fee or a maintenance reserve of roughly 1% of value annually, a vacancy allowance of 4% to 6%, management at 8% to 10% if you are not self-managing, and financing at your actual approved rate. If it does not clear after all of that, we tell you.

Financing a rental is a different product

Expect a minimum 20% down payment on a non-owner-occupied property — CMHC insurance is not available for a pure rental — and a rate typically a quarter to a half point above an owner-occupied mortgage. Lenders also apply a rental offset or add-back formula to the projected income that varies by institution and materially changes what you qualify for. Sort the financing structure before you shop.

Where the yield actually is in Regina

Three segments, in rough order of gross yield: downtown one-bedroom condos, side-by-side duplexes in Cathedral and the older core, and suited single-family homes in the newer south end. The condos produce the highest gross yield and the most condo-corporation risk. The duplexes give you separated mechanical systems and full control of the building. The suited houses give you the best resale liquidity, because you can sell them to an owner-occupier rather than only to another investor.

Inherited tenants are an asset or a liability, never neutral

Buying a tenanted property in Saskatchewan means taking on the existing tenancies under The Residential Tenancies Act, including below-market rents you cannot simply reset. Always review the leases, the rent roll, the deposit ledger and the payment history during your condition period. A property at gross rent well below market is an opportunity only if you have a realistic, lawful path to turnover.

Questions

Questions people ask

What down payment do I need for a rental property in Canada?

At least 20% for a non-owner-occupied residential property. Default insurance is not available for a pure rental, so there is no low-down-payment route. Owner-occupied properties with a suite are treated differently and can go lower.

What is a good rental yield in Regina?

Gross yields in the 7% to 9% range are achievable on downtown one-bedroom condos and older core duplexes. What matters is net yield after tax, insurance, reserve, vacancy and management — that is the number we model with you.

Can I raise the rent after buying a tenanted property?

Only within The Residential Tenancies Act: proper written notice, the required notice period, and limits on frequency. You inherit the existing tenancy and its rent. Assume the current rent for at least a year when running your numbers.
