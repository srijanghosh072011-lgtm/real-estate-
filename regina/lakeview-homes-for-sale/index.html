About Lakeview homes for sale

Why there's never anything on the market

People stay here for decades. A good share of recent sales went to buyers who planned to rebuild rather than renovate. At any given moment the whole neighbourhood might have a handful of active listings. If you want in, you wait, and then you move within days when the right one shows up. That means financing sorted well in advance.

Park proximity is the durable part

The closer a house sits to the Wascana pathway, the better its price holds in a soft market. Backing green space, or sitting two or three doors from a pathway entrance, is the most reliable value feature in Regina residential real estate. It survives rate cycles. A renovated kitchen doesn't.

Bungalows, rebuilds, and reading the listing carefully

The classic Lakeview property is a 1950s or 60s bungalow on a 60 to 75 foot lot. Plenty have been taken to the studs and rebuilt behind a kept facade, clerestory windows and stone fireplace retained, everything else new. Read the remarks closely. A renovated 1962 bungalow and a studs-out 2020 rebuild are different assets at different prices, and the MLS wording doesn't always make that obvious.

Sometimes the lot is the asset

On the bigger Lakeview lots, especially the ones over 8,000 square feet near the park, the land carries most of the value. That should change how you read condition. A tired house on a great lot can beat a tidy one on a narrow interior lot, and we'll tell you when we think that's the case.

Questions

Questions people ask

How much are homes in Lakeview, Regina?

Median's around $465,000 over a rolling year. Rebuilt houses and anything backing the pathway regularly clear $700,000. You can still find original-condition interior-lot bungalows in the high $300,000s.

Why do Lakeview homes sell so quickly?

Low turnover means few listings, and the group of buyers waiting for this neighbourhood is bigger than the supply. A well-priced Lakeview house often sees offers in the first week.

Is Lakeview good for families?

Yes, though the average age skews older than Harbour Landing. Park access, established schools and big lots suit families who'd rather have space and trees than a new build.
