Water is the first question, not the last

Drilled well, cistern with hauled water, or a rural pipeline. Each one has a different cost, a different reliability, and a different effect on financing. On a well, ask for a recent potability and flow test and find out the age and type of any treatment system. On a cistern, find out what hauling costs and how often. This is the thing buyers skip most often and the most expensive one to get wrong.

Septic, and getting it inspected properly

Most acreages run a tank with a field, a mound, or a holding tank that needs pumping. Have the system inspected specifically, because a standard home inspection usually excludes it. Ask when it was last pumped, whether it's ever backed up, and whether the field has been located and mapped. Replacing a failed field is a five-figure problem.

Shops and outbuildings, and the appraisal gap

A heated shop is often the real reason somebody wants an acreage, and it does carry value. But appraisers discount outbuildings harder than sellers expect, which can open a gap between the price you agreed and the value the appraisal comes back with. If a big shop is a large share of what you're paying for, plan for that gap ahead of time.

Acreage financing is its own conversation

Lenders treat these differently. Many will finance the house plus a limited number of acres and no more. Appraisals take longer and come back conservative. Some institutions won't lend at all without a tested potable water supply. Start with a lender who actually does acreage lending in Saskatchewan instead of assuming your existing pre-approval carries over.

Questions

Questions people ask

How far out do you have to go for an acreage near Regina?

Most acreages within a comfortable commute sit twelve to twenty-five minutes out. Pilot Butte, White City, Balgonie, Lumsden, Pense and the surrounding RMs. Past about forty minutes the commute starts running the decision.

Is it harder to get a mortgage on an acreage?

Generally yes. Lenders often finance only the house and a limited acreage, appraise conservatively, and may require tested potable water. Use a lender experienced with Saskatchewan acreages from the start.

What should I check before buying an acreage?

Water source and its test results, septic type and condition, the age and value of outbuildings, road access and who maintains it in winter, power and gas service, and the RM's tax levy and bylaws for that parcel.
